This work addresses large-scale reinforcement-learning training for 3v3 IEEE Very Small Size Soccer (VSSS) and makes three contributions. First, a JAX simulator that steps thousands of matches in parallel on a single accelerator. Its contact physics models ball spin, skidding and wall pop-ups, detects the earliest ball contact with walls, posts, robots and goal lines by swept tests, and projects coupled contacts iteratively; the referee and restart logic is shared by three environment interfaces and protected by a regression suite of collision failures. Second, a fully on-device Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) training suite with a Brand\~ao-style observation, reward and shared policy trained with mirror-symmetry data augmentation, historical self-play against a frozen opponent, and a compiled generation that contains rollout, advantage estimation and optimization. Third, an online, return-based curriculum that replays initial situations according to the returns of recent episodes inside the compiled loop. The simulator reaches \textbf{[X]} environment steps per second on \textbf{[GPU]}, against 63--378 steps per second per training agent in earlier ITAndroids ODE-based setups; the gain comes from both the simpler 2D physics and the GPU, and CPU and GPU figures are reported separately. In an ablation with equal budgets, the curriculum \textbf{[improved / did not improve]} the final policies relative to uniform random restarts (\textbf{[result]}). The main evidence is qualitative: increasingly complex behaviours (shooting, blocking, positioning for second balls, role differentiation) appear across generations. The team is also evaluated quantitatively against stationary, random and earlier-snapshot opponents and, for the last policy of each lineage, against the ITAndroids classical strategy.
